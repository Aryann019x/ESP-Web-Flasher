Getting started

Three steps, no installer, and no file ever leaves your machine.

  1. 01 · Select

    Pick the image and the board

    Use a prebuilt image from the library, or drop in your own merged .bin. The offset table in the Select panel explains what each part of an image is for.

  2. 02 · Flash

    Connect over USB and hit Flash

    Chrome or Edge talks straight to the serial port. If no port shows up, hold BOOT, tap RST, release BOOT, then try again.

  3. 03 · Verify

    Read the boot log

    Open the terminal and connect at 115200 to watch the board come up. Close the terminal before flashing — one app holds a port at a time.

Prefer the command line?
esptool.py --port COMx -b 115200 write_flash 0x00000 firmware.bin

Troubleshooting

The four things that actually go wrong, and what fixes each one.

No port appears in the picker
Hold BOOT, tap RST, release BOOT, then press Flash again. Boards without a USB-serial chip also need a data-capable cable — charge-only cables are the most common cause.
The port is busy
One application owns a serial port at a time. Close the terminal on this page first, and close any other serial monitor or IDE that may be holding the port, then reconnect.
The flash failed part-way
Reconnect at a lower baud rate, use a powered USB port rather than an unpowered hub, and make sure the image matches its offset — every prebuilt here is merged at 0x0000 (custom split files use Your .bin + Add part).
Flash succeeds but the screen stays black
Check the wiring guide — wrong display pins give a lit-but-blank screen even when the firmware boots fine. On 16MB quad-SPI S3 modules a QIO 80MHz header watchdog-loops at boot — the S3 image here is already patched to DIO 40MHz, which the SHA-256 row reflects.
The browser says Web Serial is unsupported
Web Serial needs a desktop Chromium browser — Chrome or Edge 89 and newer. Firefox, Safari and most mobile browsers do not implement it yet.
